Kamario Taylor did not take a snap. Neither did Fluff Bothwell. Both were listed questionable on Wednesday's availability report and neither appeared in the play-by-play, so Parker Puckett made his first college start in Tiger Stadium against the best defense in the SEC.

Mississippi State lost 31-0. It is the first time they have been shut out this season.

What is strange about the game is that the Bulldogs moved the ball.

## It was not turnovers

The drive chart says Mississippi State ended five possessions in a "turnover," and that is how the data labels it. It is misleading, and worth taking apart.

**Mississippi State turned the ball over exactly once** — a Puckett interception at the LSU 15 in the first quarter. No lost fumbles. LSU also lost one, a fumble after a completion. The turnover margin was even.

The other four were **turnovers on downs**:

| Quarter | Situation                | Score at the time | Result                         |
| ------- | ------------------------ | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 2nd     | 4th and 1 at the LSU 31  | 0-14              | Rush for a 6-yard loss         |
| 2nd     | 4th and 6 at the LSU 6   | 0-21              | Completion short of the sticks |
| 3rd     | 4th and 7 at the LSU 35  | 0-24              | Incomplete                     |
| 4th     | 4th and 5 at the LSU 28  | 0-31              | Incomplete                     |
| 4th     | 4th and 11 at the LSU 30 | 0-31              | Incomplete                     |

**Mississippi State went 0 for 5 on fourth down and did not attempt a single field goal.** Every one of those attempts came while trailing by fourteen or more, so going for it was defensible each time — you do not kick your way back from three scores down. But the cumulative effect is a team that got into scoring range six times and converted none of it, by a mix of execution and arithmetic.

## Where the drives died

  src="/static/images/mississippi-state-vs-lsu-downtype.png"

  width={1298}

On standard downs Mississippi State was genuinely fine: **55.1% success rate**, in line with their season figure of 54.6%. That is how a team with no quarterback and no running back gets to the opponent's 40 six times.

Then the down changed. On passing downs they managed **27.8% success and −0.57 expected points a play.**

This is the exact thing [the preview flagged](/blog/scouting-lsu): LSU's defense came in allowing 24.4% and −0.502 EPA on passing downs, the best mark in the conference. Mississippi State's offense had been 38.0% behind schedule. The result landed almost precisely on LSU's number rather than Mississippi State's, which is what happens when the better unit is the one on defense.

LSU, by contrast, was comfortable in both states — 69.8% on standard downs and **50% with +0.557 EPA on passing downs.** They were never in trouble.

Sam Leavitt's average depth of target was **2.5 yards.** Not 2.5 yards per completion — 2.5 yards past the line of scrimmage, on average, before the catch.

**76% of LSU's passing yards came after the catch** — 204 of 268 in this data. D. Jones caught eight of nine targets at an average depth of exactly zero and turned them into 58 yards. W. Watkins Jr. was targeted 0.8 yards downfield.

LSU did not beat Mississippi State over the top. They threw it at the line of scrimmage and let people run, and a defense that allowed a **65.7% success rate** could not get them down.

## The run game was not the problem

<Image
  src="/static/images/mississippi-state-vs-lsu-rush-by-week.png"
  alt="Bar chart of Mississippi State yards per carry by week. Week 1 is 9.72, then 6.21, 6.00, 4.28, 2.45 against Alabama, and 3.54 against LSU in week 7."
  width={1380}

Here is the result I did not expect. Without Bothwell, against a front that had been stuffing 26.5% of runs, Mississippi State averaged **3.54 yards a carry** — better than the 2.45 they managed against Alabama _with_ him available.

K. Wilson carried 18 times for 71. Puckett himself ran seven times for 49. The backs that my preview projected at 2.49 yards a carry against real opponents beat that figure.

The run game stopped the decline. It was everything after first down that failed.

Identical play counts, identical explosive plays, an even turnover margin, and a 31-point difference.

**Parker Puckett: 16 of 26, 121 yards, no touchdowns, one interception**, plus three sacks taken and 49 rushing yards. For a freshman making his first start on the road against the SEC's best defense, that is not a disgrace. It is also 4.7 yards an attempt, and an offense that averages 4.7 an attempt does not score.

## What this does and does not tell us

Mississippi State falls to 4-2. The sensible caution is that this result is close to uninterpretable as a measure of the team: they played without the quarterback who had thrown sixteen touchdowns to four interceptions and the back who had taken 84% of their carries.

What it does tell us is narrower and still useful. The offense that has carried this team all season was carrying a specific quarterback, and the parts around him — an offensive line that produced 3.54 a carry, receivers who got open on early downs, a defense that stuffed runs at 18% — were good enough to reach the opponent's 40 six times against the best defense in the SEC.

They were not good enough to finish once. That is the gap a quarterback fills.
