Mississippi State won its SEC opener 41-34 at South Carolina to go 3-0. The box score says shootout. The shape of the game says something more specific: for one half the Bulldogs barely had the ball, and for most of the next one South Carolina couldn't keep it.

## The first half: 19 snaps

Mississippi State ran **19 plays in the entire first half.** South Carolina ran 47.

That is the single most surprising number in this game, and it explains the early scoreboard better than anything else. MSU's first three possessions were an interception, a three-and-out, and a punt. South Carolina, meanwhile, went field goal, touchdown, field goal, field goal.

Read that top to bottom and the game tells itself.

**The defense's best work came on the first drive.** South Carolina took the opening possession 12 plays and 76 yards to first-and-goal at the MSU 5. Three runs gained four yards — the last of them a one-yard loss on third-and-goal from the one — and the Gamecocks settled for a 30-yard field goal. That was the pattern for the half: South Carolina reached scoring position on five of its six drives and came away with **three field goals and two touchdowns**, not five touchdowns.

Holding an offense to field goals is not a moral victory. It was the difference between trailing 23-14 and trailing by a lot more.

## The second quarter: 7 plays, 14 points

Mississippi State's answer wasn't a long, grinding drive. It was two of them that barely qualified as drives at all.

| Drive       | Plays | Yards | How it went                                                                                       |
| ----------- | ----- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2nd Q, 8:55 | **3** | 75    | Bothwell 43-yard run, Bothwell 2, Taylor 30-yard touchdown to S. Magee                            |
| 2nd Q, 6:27 | **4** | 68    | Two runs, a third-and-1 converted 16 yards to A. Evans III, Taylor 43-yard touchdown to Z. Ragins |

Fourteen points on seven snaps. The touchdown throws travelled 30 and 43 yards, and neither drive ran a single snap inside the South Carolina 20.

This is what an offense that isn't getting possessions has to look like, and it is also why the first-half efficiency numbers are so strange: **MSU averaged 10.0 yards per play before halftime and still trailed by nine.**

The tempting story is that the offense woke up. The numbers don't really say that — the offense was already good. Look at what moved:

|                            | 1st half | 2nd half   |
| -------------------------- | -------- | ---------- |
| **MSU snaps**              | 19       | **38**     |
| **South Carolina snaps**   | 47       | **32**     |
| South Carolina third downs | 4 of 10  | **2 of 8** |
| MSU average drive start    | own 20   | **own 48** |
| MSU EPA per play           | +0.36    | +0.38      |

Mississippi State's efficiency was almost identical across the two halves. What changed is that it doubled its number of snaps, started drives 28 yards closer to the end zone, and got South Carolina off the field on third down.

Two interceptions did most of that work. J. Smith picked Sellers off at the South Carolina 44 and D. Riley did it again at the South Carolina 36, and both were returned. South Carolina's first four possessions after halftime: a three-and-out that lost five yards, an interception, an interception, another three-and-out.

Mississippi State scored on **five straight drives** to open the second half — touchdown, touchdown, field goal, touchdown, field goal — and turned a nine-point deficit into an eighteen-point lead.

**Kamario Taylor: 18 of 26, 251 yards, 4 touchdowns, 1 interception.** He also ran four times for a team-best +7.0 EPA, including a 17-yard scramble on third-and-14 from his own 21.

**LaNorris Sellers: 25 of 40, 282 yards, 2 touchdowns, 2 interceptions.** South Carolina got its yards — 79 plays' worth — but averaged 5.0 a snap and needed 18 third downs.

Fluff Bothwell carried 21 times for 115 yards. Anthony Evans III caught all six of his targets. And Mississippi State's fifth touchdown pass wasn't thrown by its quarterback: **G. Harris threw the only pass of his season, seven yards to S. West**, on the first play of the fourth quarter.

## The fourth quarter

Leading 41-23, Mississippi State did not put the game away so much as refuse to let go of it. After the touchdown that opened the quarter, its last two drives were a field goal and a three-and-out that lost six yards. South Carolina scored 11 points in the final quarter — a touchdown with a two-point conversion, then a 52-yard field goal with 56 seconds left.

It got to 41-34 and no closer. The onside situation never arrived.

One thing worth correcting, since it's the fear this game invites: **Mississippi State did not have a fourth-quarter collapse problem last season.** In 2025 the Bulldogs led entering the fourth quarter six times and lost once — at home to Tennessee, up 24-20, in a game that finished 41-34 the other way. Across the twelve 2025 games where the drive data reconciles with the final score, they outscored opponents 107-64 in fourth quarters. The story of 2025 wasn't blown leads; it was games that were already gone by the fourth.

What this one showed is narrower and more useful: leading a conference road game by 18 with a quarter to play, they gave up a touchdown and a field goal and won by seven.
