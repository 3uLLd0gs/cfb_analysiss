Two weeks is not much football. Before looking at any numbers, here's how to read them.

- **Only games against FBS opponents count.** Half the league played an FCS team in the first two weeks, so eight SEC teams are working from a single game.
- **Garbage time is removed**, using the [same rule as always](/blog/the-plays-that-dont-count). Some of these teams got to garbage time very early.
- **Success rate is the number to trust most.** It doesn't depend on expected points, and one long play can't swing it the way it swings yards per play. Look at the **Plays** column before believing any row.

- **Georgia and South Carolina top the table on 25 and 35 plays.** Georgia's game against Western Kentucky reached garbage time in the second quarter, and South Carolina's against Kent State in the third. It's a good start and a tiny sample.

Turnovers are expensive in expected points, and LSU still won both games by 30-plus.
- **Kentucky's only FBS game was Alabama, and Arkansas's was a trip to Utah.** Those numbers say as much about the opponent as the offense.

---

_How these numbers are built: scrimmage plays only, with kneels and spikes removed, against FBS opponents. Garbage time uses Bill Connelly's quarter-by-quarter margins. Success rate: a play succeeds if it gains 50% of the distance on first down, 70% on second, or 100% on third or fourth, and turnovers always fail. Yards per play leaves out turnover return yardage, which the raw data credits to the offense. Some plays in the 2026 data have unreliable expected-points values, so they are left out of EPA but kept everywhere else. Targets are complete and incomplete passes to a named receiver. Data from cfbfastR._
