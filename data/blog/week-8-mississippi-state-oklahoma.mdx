Mississippi State got both of them back. Kamario Taylor started. Fluff Bothwell played the whole game and ran for 111 yards at 6.5 a carry, his best day since September.

They lost 23-15, to a team that missed three field goals, while converting **one third down out of eleven.**

## Taylor lasted a quarter

Taylor took the first snap and played Mississippi State's first three possessions. His afternoon: **2 of 5 for 10 yards, and three sacks taken on eight dropbacks.** He did not appear again after the 4:49 mark of the first quarter.

| Mississippi State offense  | Plays | Yards per play | Success rate | EPA per play |
| -------------------------- | ----- | -------------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| First quarter, Taylor      | 12    | 4.00           | 17%          | **−0.63**    |
| Second quarter on, Puckett | 50    | 6.06           | 40%          | **+0.08**    |

That table is not an argument that the freshman is better than the starter. It is a description of a quarterback who was listed questionable for two straight weeks, got sacked on three of his eight dropbacks, and came out. Parker Puckett finished **17 of 24 for 158 yards with a touchdown and an interception** in relief, and the offense functioned better with him in it.

**6.36 yards a carry**, their best figure since the opener against UL Monroe. Bothwell carried 17 times for 111 with a 37-yard run and a 65% opportunity rate. Puckett added 37 yards on five carries.

The run game that declined every week from September through the Alabama game is fixed. It did not matter.

## One for eleven

On standard downs Mississippi State was the better team: **47.5% success rate against Oklahoma's 34.0%**, and positive expected points per play. They out-gained Oklahoma on early downs all afternoon.

Then the down count got to three, and the offense stopped existing. **13.6% success rate on passing downs, −0.45 expected points a play.** Oklahoma, facing the same situation, managed 33.3% and +0.47.

One conversion in eleven third-down attempts. That is the game.

It is also the second week running. Against LSU the split was 55.1% on standard downs and [27.8% behind schedule](/blog/week-7-mississippi-state-lsu). Against Oklahoma it was 47.5% and 13.6%. Two different quarterbacks, two different defenses, the same shape: a team that moves the ball until the situation demands a specific throw.

Oklahoma reached the Mississippi State 40 **eight times** to Mississippi State's four. That is the difference between 469 yards and 351.

And Oklahoma was not efficient with it. Tate Sandell made three field goals and **missed three** — from 45, 45 and 41 yards. Nine points left on the grass by a team that won by eight.

## The two giveaways

Mississippi State turned it over twice: a Puckett interception in the third quarter, and a **fumble at the Oklahoma 4-yard line** in the second, on a Puckett run that had just picked up two yards to the goal line's doorstep.

That fumble is worth a sentence on its own. Mississippi State had four trips inside the Oklahoma 40 all game. One of them ended on the 4 without a point.

## What Oklahoma did

John Mateer went 21 of 28 for 247 yards in this data, throwing at an average depth of 8.7 yards. Isaiah Sategna III caught seven of ten targets for 128 and was the problem all afternoon — Mississippi State allowed **six plays of 20+ yards** and generated two.

The Bulldogs' pass rush produced one sack against Oklahoma's four. That is the other half of the third-down story: Mississippi State could not get off the field, and could not stay on it.

## Where this leaves them

Mississippi State is 4-3, having lost three straight to Alabama, LSU and Oklahoma by a combined score of 110-38.

The diagnosis has changed over those three weeks, which is worth saying plainly. Against Alabama the problem was a defensive front that never won. Against LSU it was an offense without its two best players. Against Oklahoma both of those excuses got weaker — the run game produced 6.36 a carry, Bothwell was healthy, the defense held a good offense to 33.8% success — and they still lost, because they could not convert a third down.
