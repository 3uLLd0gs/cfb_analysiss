Mississippi State started its third different quarterback in three weeks, gained 2.97 yards a play, and lost 31-3 in Austin. It is the fourth straight loss, and the second time in four weeks the offense has failed to reach double figures.

The strange part is that they were not beaten on field position, and they were not beaten on opportunities.

|                                | Mississippi State | Texas    |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------- | -------- |
| Average drive start            | **own 37.5**      | own 23.0 |
| Drives                         | 14                | 11       |
| Trips inside the opponent's 40 | **5**             | 3        |
| Points from those trips        | **3**             | 17       |
| Share of available yards       | 22.1%             | 33.2%    |

Mississippi State started its drives **fourteen and a half yards closer to the end zone than Texas did**, reached scoring range more often, and lost by 28.

## A third quarterback

Kamario Taylor did not play. A.J. Swann — who had not taken a snap all season, and who I noted in [the LSU preview](/blog/scouting-lsu) was impossible to evaluate because he had no data — started and played the first half. Parker Puckett took over late in the second quarter and finished the game.

|                          | Att | Comp  | Yards | TD / INT | Sacks taken |
| ------------------------ | --- | ----- | ----- | -------- | ----------- |
| A. Swann (first half)    | 16  | 12    | 97    | 0 / 0    | 1           |
| P. Puckett (second half) | 10  | **3** | 22    | 0 / 0    | **5**       |

Swann completed twelve of sixteen and moved the ball in short increments. Puckett completed three of ten and was sacked five times.

**Texas is credited with seven sacks**, the highest total Mississippi State has allowed this season. The play-by-play attributes five of them to Puckett and one to Swann cleanly; the rest are tangled up in a fumble and a penalty, which is why the two rows above do not add to seven.

## One of Texas's touchdowns was scored by its defense

This is the detail that makes the scoreboard misleading. Texas scored 31 points:

- Three Arch Manning touchdown passes
- One 45-yard field goal
- **One defensive touchdown** — Swann was strip-sacked near the end of the first half and Texas returned it for the score

Texas's offense, taken on its own, was not good. **5.2 yards a play, a 37.0% success rate, and −0.131 EPA per snap.** Both offenses were below average on the day. Only one of them had a defense that scored.

There was nearly a second one. With 3:09 left in the second quarter, Texas returned a punt 84 yards for a touchdown — and it came back on a penalty. The play is still in the data as a punt return touchdown; the scoreboard never moved. If it had stood, this is 38-3.

## This loss was different from the last two

Against LSU and Oklahoma, Mississippi State had a specific, identifiable failure: they moved the ball fine on early downs and died on third. Against LSU it was 55.1% on standard downs and 27.8% behind schedule. Against Oklahoma, 47.5% and 13.6%.

Against Texas there was no such split. **28.2% on standard downs and 19.2% on passing downs**, with negative expected points in both states. The offense did not fall apart at a particular moment in the down sequence. It never worked at all.

That is a worse diagnosis than the previous two weeks, not a better one. A team that is good on first down and bad on third has something to fix. A team that is bad on every down has something to rebuild.

Fourteen possessions, six punts, three three-and-outs, one field goal and no touchdowns. Mississippi State gained **193 yards on 65 plays**, and 22.1% of the yards available to them.

Texas was not much busier — eleven drives, six punts, five three-and-outs of their own. They simply converted three of their chances into touchdowns and Mississippi State converted none.

## The run game went backwards again

Two weeks ago the run game looked fixed: 6.36 a carry against Oklahoma with Fluff Bothwell back. Against Texas it was **3.40**, with Bothwell carrying 16 times for 55 yards and a 31% opportunity rate.

One good week against Oklahoma and one bad one against Texas is not a trend in either direction. What it does mean is that the thing I called fixed two weeks ago was not yet fixed.

A 24.6% success rate is the worst figure Mississippi State has posted this season by a wide margin. One explosive play in sixty-five snaps is the other way of saying the same thing.

## Where the season is

Mississippi State is 4-4. The four losses have come to Alabama, LSU, Oklahoma and Texas by a combined **141 to 41**, and the quarterback who ranks [third in this conference by adjusted EPA per play](/blog/ranking-sec-quarterbacks) has played one quarter of the last three games.

It is worth being careful about what that does and does not establish. Those four opponents are, collectively, the hardest stretch on the schedule, and Mississippi State has played most of it without the player its offense was built around. Both things are true, and neither of them turns a 2.97-yard average into a good day.

_How these numbers are built: scrimmage plays only, kneels and spikes removed; Mississippi State ran 12 garbage-time snaps and Texas 4, and the headline rates hold with them removed. Success rate: a play succeeds if it gains 50% of the needed yards on first down, 70% on second, or 100% on third or fourth; turnovers always fail. Standard downs are first down, second-and-7 or less, and third or fourth and 4 or less. "Giveaways" counts interceptions and lost fumbles only, separating them from turnovers on downs. Box score lines were checked against ESPN and match on the final score, both quarterbacks, the sack counts both ways, and the turnover totals. Two things in the raw data need care and are handled here: Texas's 84-yard punt return is recorded as a punt return touchdown although a penalty wiped it out and the score never changed, and Texas's defensive touchdown contributes no offensive EPA, which is why a team that scored 31 shows a negative figure. Small differences against ESPN's passing totals (one attempt each way) come from the missing-play gaps this data carries in most 2026 games. Data from cfbfastR._
